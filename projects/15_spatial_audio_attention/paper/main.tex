\begin{abstract}
\noindent Service robots often hear through a small circular microphone
array but use it as a single microphone. We study what the four raw
microphones of a ReSpeaker USB v2.0 (6.4\,cm aperture) can add to a
robot's speech pipeline: the talker's angle, the talker's distance, and
the suppression of sound from other directions before neural denoising.
The common thread is a \emph{silence model}: the pitch-based voice
activity detector (VAD) the pipeline already runs splits the stream into
voice and silence, and the silence frames give the cross-spectra of
everything that is not the talker. In image-source simulations with a
voiced talker at 0\,dB SNR and a directional fan, plain SRP-PHAT has a
median per-frame error of \doafansrpplainsnrZerortThreeMed/\doafansrpplainsnrZerortSixMed$^\circ$
(RT$_{60}$ 0.3/0.6\,s); subtracting the silence cross-spectra and
weighting bins by speech presence brings it to
\doafansrpsnrZerortThreeMed/\doafansrpsnrZerortSixMed$^\circ$, and a
streaming tracker follows a moving talker with a median error of
\demoAzMedErr$^\circ$. Distance cannot come from time differences on this
aperture; the coherent-to-diffuse ratio is the useful cue, but it is
room-relative: cross-room error is \distxroomcdrlevel\,m without and
\distxroomcdrrtSixZero\,m with the room's RT$_{60}$ (predicting the mean:
\distPredictMean\,m). An MVDR beamformer steered at the estimated talker
improves output SINR against a competing talker by \beamMvdrMean\,dB on
average. A first hardware session in a real room changes two things. The
raw microphones carry low-frequency hum that the simulation lacked
(\hwNoiseLowPct\,\% of the noise power below 100\,Hz); it broke the VAD
(\hwLiveZeroSpeech{} speech frames in \hwLiveZeroSec\,s of talking) until
its input was high-passed at 200\,Hz. The simulation-trained distance
model does not transfer, so we replace it with a microphone-only
near/mid/far estimate that learns its reference from the session's own
utterances; it separates near from far talkers but moves with vocal
effort. Code, live radar and hardware tools are released.
\end{abstract}
