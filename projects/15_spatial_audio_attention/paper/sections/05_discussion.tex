\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The silence model is the method.} The estimators are textbook.
What made them work on this array was getting ``noise'' right: which bins
contain it, which frames it can be learned from (a fixed energy gate tuned
for the processed channel missed a talker at 3\,m entirely), and when old
statistics stop describing the scene (a talker who moved between
utterances). The hardware session made the same point a fourth time: the
real room's hum, absent from the simulation, decided which frames were
``voice'', and every downstream estimate inherits that decision. A
high-pass on the VAD input fixed it on hardware and cost one reverberant
clip in simulation; the cut-off is a property of the deployment's noise,
not of the method.

\paragraph{Distance is a room-calibrated quantity.} A 6.4\,cm array has no
geometric range resolution; the CDR measures distance relative to the
room's critical distance. Simulation predicts and the cross-room
experiment confirms that a room descriptor is needed, and the real room
was outside the simulated range. A robot that changes rooms cannot be
calibrated per room, so the microphone-only output we keep is relative:
near, mid or far compared with what the robot has heard in this session.
This is honest about what one small array knows, but it inherits the
cue's confounds. The speech level confuses distance with vocal effort; the
CDR does not depend on effort but on the room and on SNR. In simulation,
1\,m to 3\,m spans only about 7.5\,dB of speech level, so three bands are
separable only for distance changes of about a factor two. Other sensors
are the natural source of the missing reference: a person detector with
depth gives distance labels whenever it sees the talker.

\paragraph{Where simulation is optimistic.} The synthetic talker is more
periodic and spectrally simpler than speech, which helps both the VAD and
PHAT; the shoebox image-source model has specular reflections and no
furniture, scattering or robot body; the diffuse field is ideal; there is no
low-frequency hum; and the microphones are perfectly matched and positioned.
The first two showed on hardware: the room was more reverberant than any
simulated one and the noise was hum-dominated.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The simulation sweeps are small (8 clips per DOA
cell, 108 distance clips, 8 beamforming scenes) and have no confidence
intervals; a larger configuration is provided. The hardware comes from two
sessions of one talker in one room. The labelled angles were approximate,
and the firmware DOA was not read on Windows. The distance clips span only
0.4--2\,m. There is one interferer at a time, no moving interferer and no
own-voice (TTS) handling.
