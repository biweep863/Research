\section{Conclusion}

On a four-microphone, 6.4\,cm array, the voice/silence split a robot's
speech pipeline already computes is enough to turn the raw channels into
a usable sense of where the talker is, provided the split is right. In
simulation: a few degrees of median angle error at 0\,dB SNR when the
silence frames are used both to subtract noise and to weight frequency
bins, distance within about a third of a metre once the room's
reverberation is known, and several dB of suppression of a competing
talker with an MVDR filter steered at the estimate. On hardware, the
direction followed the talker once the VAD was protected from the room's
hum, while metric distance did not transfer to an unseen room; a relative
near/mid/far estimate that learns from the session itself is what the
microphones alone can offer. Next steps are the labelled hardware grid
(angle error against the firmware DOA, and labelled distances to choose the
range cue), a two-talker beamforming test, and integration as ROS~2 nodes
ahead of the existing DeepFilterNet3 denoiser, where a person detector can
supply the distance reference the array lacks.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} \texttt{make all} in
\texttt{projects/15\_spatial\_audio\_attention} regenerates every
simulation number and figure in about four minutes on a laptop CPU;
\texttt{make hw} regenerates the hardware numbers from the session's raw
logs (kept locally; the derived JSON is in the repository), and
\texttt{make live}, \texttt{make record} and \texttt{make calibrate} run
the hardware study.
