\paragraph{Angle.} Figure~\ref{fig:doa}. At 10--20\,dB SNR the full
method is within $2^\circ$. At 0\,dB the two uses of the silence model
matter in different conditions (pooled per-clip MAE, RT$_{60}$ 0.3/0.6\,s;
per-frame medians in brackets). With diffuse noise, dropping the
speech-presence weights raises the error from
\doadiffusesrpsnrZerortThree/\doadiffusesrpsnrZerortSix$^\circ$
(\doadiffusesrpsnrZerortThreeMed/\doadiffusesrpsnrZerortSixMed) to
\doadiffusesrpnosnrwsnrZerortThree/\doadiffusesrpnosnrwsnrZerortSix$^\circ$:
after subtraction, noise-only bins carry structured residue that PHAT
normalises to full weight. With the fan, plain SRP-PHAT locks on the fan
(\doafansrpplainsnrZerortThree/\doafansrpplainsnrZerortSix$^\circ$, medians
\doafansrpplainsnrZerortThreeMed/\doafansrpplainsnrZerortSixMed), while
the full method keeps a median of
\doafansrpsnrZerortThreeMed/\doafansrpsnrZerortSixMed$^\circ$. The
MAE of \doadiffusesrpsnrZerortSix/\doafansrpsnrZerortSix$^\circ$ at
RT$_{60}$ 0.6\,s comes from a single clip per cell, and from the VAD
high-pass added for the hardware (Table~\ref{tab:vadhp}): without it the
same cells were 3.0/2.6$^\circ$. GCC-PHAT with least squares reaches
\doadiffusegccsnrZerortThree--\doafangccsnrZerortSix$^\circ$ at 0\,dB. The
$F_0$-harmonic comb changes nothing measurable on our synthetic talker,
whose spectrum is already purely harmonic. At 0\,dB, 7 of the 8 clips per
cell have any VAD-speech frame.

changes by only a few dB.

cue ($\rho=\rhocdrdb$ pooled, \rhoCdrRoomMax{} to \rhoCdrRoomMin{} within a room). Level

\subsection{Hardware session}
\label{sec:hw}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/hw_session.pdf}
\caption{Hardware session. Left: noise spectrum on the raw microphones in
a real room (silence of a labelled clip), with the VAD's $F_0$ search band
and the two high-pass cut-offs tried. Right: per-utterance cues in an
\hwLiveMin-minute live session (\hwLiveUtt{} utterances), coloured by the
band the online speech-level estimate assigned.}
\label{fig:hw}
\end{figure*}

A ReSpeaker USB v2.0 (6-channel firmware) on a laptop in a home office;
the firmware estimates RT$_{60}$\,=\,\hwFwRtSixty\,s, beyond the simulated
rooms. Data: one labelled clip (front, 0.5\,m), a short live log with the
simulation's VAD and an \hwLiveMin-minute live log with the final
configuration, in which the talker moved between about 1\,m, close and
about 3\,m without labels.

\paragraph{The VAD failed first.} The noise floor is
\hwNoiseDb\,dBFS with \hwNoiseLowPct\,\% of its power below 100\,Hz
(Figure~\ref{fig:hw}, left); speech raises the broadband level by less than
1\,dB. With the simulation's VAD, \hwLiveZeroSpeech{} of
\hwLiveZeroFrames{} frames were speech during \hwLiveZeroSec\,s of talking,
so the radar never produced a distance. The angle from those few frames was
stable: SRP-PHAT works in 200--3800\,Hz, above the hum. Table~\ref{tab:hwvad}:
a high-pass on the VAD input restores coverage; at 150\,Hz the hum's
harmonics still pass the pitch test in silence, at 200\,Hz they do not. With
200\,Hz, \hwLiveSpeechPct\,\% of the long session's frames are voiced
speech.

\paragraph{The distance model does not transfer.} On the 0.5\,m clip the
CDR is \hwClipCdr\,dB (simulation at 0.5\,m: about $+7$ to $+10$\,dB) at an SNR
of \hwClipSnr\,dB, and the simulation-trained model reads
\hwClipModelRange\,m. This is the room dependence of
Figure~\ref{fig:distfeat} in a more reverberant room than any simulated
one, plus a quieter talker.

\paragraph{Online near/mid/far.} In the long session the speech-level
estimate spent \hwBandspeechdbNear/\hwBandspeechdbMid/\hwBandspeechdbFar\,\%
of utterances near/mid/far and changed band between consecutive utterances
\hwSwitchSpeech\,\% of the time. Replaying the same utterances with the CDR
as the cue gives \hwBandcdrdbNear/\hwBandcdrdbMid/\hwBandcdrdbFar\,\% and
\hwSwitchCdr\,\% changes. The two cues agree on \hwBandAgree\,\% of
utterances and are only weakly related (Spearman \hwRhoSpeechCdr;
Figure~\ref{fig:hw}, right). In use, near and far were told apart, and
speaking louder or softer moved the band. Without distance labels we cannot
say which cue is right more often.
