\section{Conclusion}
Both GPT-2 and Pythia-2.8B carry a linearly decodable representation of
logical validity in their middle layers, on both a natural-language and
an invented, out-of-distribution surface. But scale does not make that
representation more surface-form-invariant -- cross-surface transfer is
weaker at 2.8B than at 124M -- and it does not concentrate ablation
effects into an identifiable logic circuit either; if anything, the
opposite on both counts. This contradicts the prevailing view that
mechanistic specialisation and cross-context generalisation should both
improve with scale, at least for this probe-and-ablate methodology on
this task, and is reported as such rather than reconciled with that
prior by construction.
