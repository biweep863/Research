\title{Mechanistic Probes of Logical-Symbolic Reasoning in Small Transformers:\\
Evidence for a Surface-Form-Invariant Validity Direction}
\author[1]{Luis Benvenuto}
\affil[1]{Data Science \& Mathematics, Independent Research}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent We probe whether a small autoregressive transformer (GPT-2) carries
an internal representation of \emph{logical validity} that is decoupled from
natural-language surface form. To control for memorisation, we construct
parallel English and invented-conlang versions of five logical templates
(modus ponens, modus tollens, Barbara syllogism, AND-chain, OR-chain) and
report two diagnostics: (i) per-layer linear probes for validity, and (ii)
exhaustive attention-head ablations. Probes reach
$\gptTwoProbeEnPeak$ accuracy at layer $\gptTwoProbeEnPeakLayer$ on English
and $\gptTwoProbeClPeak$ at layer $\gptTwoProbeClPeakLayer$ on the conlang;
cross-surface transfer reaches $\gptTwoCrossEnCl$ (English $\to$ conlang),
clearly above the $0.5$ chance level despite the conlang vocabulary lying
outside pretraining distribution. Head-ablation flags a single early head
(layer $\gptTwoWorstHeadLayer$, head $\gptTwoWorstHeadIndex$) whose
removal hurts logic accuracy by $\gptTwoWorstHeadDeltaAcc$ while
simultaneously inflating LM cross-entropy by $\gptTwoWorstHeadDeltaLM$,
suggesting it is more than a logic-specific component. We discuss methodological implications for
mechanistic interpretability of reasoning.
\end{abstract}
